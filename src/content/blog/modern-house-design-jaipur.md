A modern house is more than flat roofs and large windows. In Jaipur, where summers are long and the sun is strong, good modern design starts with the climate and then finds a clean, contemporary way to answer it.

## Orientation comes first

How the house sits on the plot decides how hot it gets. Living spaces and large openings work best facing north, where the light is soft and steady. West-facing walls take the harshest afternoon sun, so they suit stairs, stores, toilets and service areas, or need deep shading.

## Shade before glass

Large glass walls look modern, but without shade they turn a room into a greenhouse. Deep overhangs, timber or metal louvres, perforated screens and recessed windows keep the glare and heat out while letting light in. Many of the best contemporary facades in Rajasthan are really shading systems, a modern take on the jaali.

## Courtyards and cross-ventilation

A courtyard or a double-height space pulls light into the middle of the house and lets hot air rise and escape. Openings on opposite sides of a room give cross-breezes in the evening. These old ideas from havelis work just as well in a modern plan.

## Materials that suit the place

Local stone, lime and textured plaster age well and keep interiors cooler than thin, glossy finishes. Exposed concrete, timber and metal give a crisp modern look when they are detailed carefully. Fewer materials, used well, usually look more refined than many.

## Green terraces and planting

Planted terraces, roof gardens and green walls cool the rooms below, soften a hard facade and give you outdoor space on a small plot. Plan the waterproofing, drainage and irrigation at the design stage, not afterwards.

## Front elevation

The front elevation is what the street sees, but it should follow the plan rather than be added on top. When the elevation grows out of the shading, the balconies and the planting, it looks calm and lasts longer than a facade made of trends.
