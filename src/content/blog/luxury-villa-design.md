A luxury villa is not defined by how much marble or gold it has. The homes that feel most luxurious are calm, generous and carefully made. Here is what we focus on when designing a villa or bungalow.

## Space and proportion

Generous ceiling heights, a double-height living room, wide passages and rooms in good proportion make a house feel open. Luxury is often about the space that is not filled.

## Light

Daylight changes through the day and makes simple materials look rich. Skylights, courtyards, tall windows with deep shade and well-placed artificial lighting give a villa its atmosphere at every hour.

## Indoors and outdoors

A villa should open to its garden. Verandas, terraces, a pool court and planted courtyards extend the living space outside and make the most of the plot.

## Materials

Natural stone, solid timber, brass, lime plaster and textured finishes age well and feel good to touch. A small palette of fine materials looks more refined than many competing ones.

## Detail

What separates a luxury home is the detail: how stone meets timber, how doors close, where lights and switches sit, how storage is hidden. These are drawn and checked before construction, not decided on site.

## Architecture, interiors and landscape together

When one studio designs the building, the interiors, the furniture and the landscape, the villa reads as one idea. Views line up with the garden, lighting follows the furniture layout, and nothing feels added on.

## Privacy and service

Separate entries and routes for staff, a discreet service kitchen, and private family areas away from guest spaces are what make a large home easy to live in.
