Many families in Jaipur want their new home to follow Vastu. A modern design and a Vastu-compliant plan are not in conflict: most Vastu principles are about light, air and sensible zoning, which good architecture cares about too.

## Tell your architect at the start

Vastu is easiest when it guides the plan from the first sketch. Adding it after the design is done means moving kitchens, toilets and staircases, which usually makes the house worse. If you follow Vastu, or have a Vastu consultant, bring that into the first meeting.

## Common priorities

- **Entrance.** The main door position is often the first thing families ask about. It depends on which side the road is, so the plot's orientation matters.
- **Kitchen.** Usually placed in the south-east, with the cook facing east where possible.
- **Master bedroom.** Often in the south-west of the house.
- **Puja room.** Usually in the north-east, a quiet and well-lit part of the plan.
- **Open centre.** Keeping the middle of the house open or light-filled, which a courtyard or double-height space does well.

## Where Vastu and good design agree

North-east light is soft and steady, which suits a puja room or a living space. Placing heavy, closed rooms in the south-west shields the house from the harsh afternoon sun. Much of traditional Vastu reads like good climate design for Rajasthan.

## When the plot doesn't fit

Not every plot faces the ideal direction. A good architect can still work with what you have, through the arrangement of rooms, the entrance and the open spaces, instead of forcing a layout that does not suit the site.
